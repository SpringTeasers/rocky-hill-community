Welcome to Rocky Hill

Welcome to Rocky Hill, Connecticut

Rocky Hill is a small town with a long story. Settled along the Connecticut River, home to world-famous dinosaur tracks, and just eight miles south of Hartford, it is a place where neighbours still know each other’s names.

Whether you have lived here for decades or you are planning your first visit, this site is a starting point: what to do, where to eat and shop, and how to reach your town government.

Dinosaur tracks in our backyard

Rocky Hill is home to Dinosaur State Park, where thousands of Early Jurassic dinosaur footprints were discovered and are preserved under a protective dome. It is one of the largest dinosaur track sites in North America and is the reason a dinosaur appears on so much of our local identity.

A river town with a working history

Because Rocky Hill sits on the Connecticut River, it began as a natural port for neighbouring Wethersfield — an early centre for shipbuilding, agriculture and trade. The town takes its name from the ridge along the river in the northeast, now part of Rocky Hill Quarry Park.

Wikipedia ↗

Small town, close to everything

Rocky Hill has grown from about 1,000 residents in 1850 to more than 20,000 today, and has held on to a relaxed, residential feel. Many residents commute to Hartford or New Haven, which is why Rocky Hill is often described as a bedroom community — but with its own parks, events and town centre on the Silas Deane Highway.
